Population by age: follow the evidence

ONS bundle homepage · Open the Nomis metadata record in Explorer · Open the source definition concept in Explorer

This walkthrough follows a realistic question through the frozen ONS metadata bundle. It shows what the source records, which nearby records can be confused with it, and where a live query still needs decisions.

The question

I need a population trend by age for a named local authority and a stated period. Which ONS dataset and edition should I consider? What nearby series could be confused with it? Which geography vintage, provenance and quality evidence can I inspect, and what must be chosen before a live query is authorised?

Candidate supported by the frozen source

Nomis NM_2002_1

Population estimates - local authority based by single year of age. The source describes mid-year estimates for 30 June, based on the latest Census of Population with allowance for under-enumeration, available at local-authority level and above.

The record lists six dimensions: age, frequency, gender, geography, measures and time. It records first release on 11 August 2010 and revision on 30 July 2025. Its geography-vintage, frequency, edition and version fields are blank or absent in this snapshot; the walkthrough does not fill them in.

It links to the ONS mid-year population estimates quality and methods information. The source record has no separate methodology link. The QMI link is recorded as evidence; this walkthrough does not revalidate its live contents.

Negative controls and nearby records

NM_31_1: five-year age band

The title shares population and local-authority wording, but the age grain differs. It must not stand in for a single-year series when the question requires that level of detail.

NM_2010_1: 2011-based small area

This is a different geographic grain and vintage, despite also describing single-year age estimates.

NM_2014_1: 2021-based small area

The title identifies a 2021-based small-area series. It is not silently substituted for the local-authority candidate.

ONS Data API ageing series

ageing-population-estimates is on a different source surface and has different edition, version, frequency and unit metadata.

Similarity is not equivalence. The graph assertions are inferred and marked discovery-only. They do not establish statistical identity, suitability or endorsement.

Where the answer stops

The local MCP plan is non-executing. It does not return observations or validate live options. A separate accountable service must check the dataset edition, geography and vintage, age, sex, measure, frequency and period against its live schema, then confirm purpose and authority before any retrieval.

No paid language model is used in the saved broker replay. The replay tests metadata access and plan construction; it does not test an AI client's answer quality.
